# Apollo

**Open your world, and let other writers grow it with you.**

You build a world once. Other writers tell their own stories inside it.
The lore stays consistent, the revenue splits itself, and readers help decide which stories become canon.

## Why it exists

Every good world attracts other stories. Right now, that always gets awkward.

- A fan writes a spin-off, and the **agreement with the original creator is just a conversation**
- The moment it earns money, **it becomes a dispute**
- Someone breaks the lore, and **there is no way to stop it**
- New readers hit **unfamiliar names** and quietly leave

Apollo makes that relationship explicit from the start.

## Who it's for

**The world creator**
Organize your lore as a wiki, lock the rules nobody may break, and decide who gets in.
You also set what share of the revenue from your world comes back to you.

**The writers inside it**
Start in a world that is already alive instead of a blank page — with readers who already know it.
No digging through documents: the wiki sits open right beside the manuscript.

**The readers**
Tap an unfamiliar name and see what it means, right there.
Vote to push a story you love into canon. Even if you never write a word, you can propose new lore — and earn contribution points when it's adopted.

## How it works

**1. Open a world**
Add characters, places, events, and terms to the wiki. Give an event a date and you get a timeline; give a place coordinates and you get a map.

**2. Set the rules**
Lock a rule like "no magic, ever" and register the words that break it. When a writer types one, the editor quietly says so. It never blocks them — it just tells them.

**3. Decide who's in**
Open the world to everyone, or keep it private and invite people in.
Invitations come in two levels: **viewers** read and propose, **editors** help run the wiki.

**4. Writers write**
World wiki on the left, manuscript on the right. Stop typing and it saves itself.
Use a name from the wiki in your text and it links automatically, so readers can look it up while they read.

**5. Readers decide canon**
Spin-offs that resonate go through a vote and become official.
The ones that don't aren't thrown away — they stay as parallel-universe (IF) stories and keep finding readers.

## What makes it different

**Lore comes first.** A writer knows they've broken a rule before they publish, not after.

**Revenue splits aren't negotiated.** They follow the share you set when you created the world.
Settlement records are append-only, so there's never an argument later about who was paid what.
The moment someone agreed to use your world — and the terms at that time — is recorded too.

**Newcomers can read it.** Tap an unfamiliar name and the explanation appears in place.

**You can take part without writing.** Adopted lore proposals credit you as a contributor and earn points.

**The world can't be wrecked.** Every wiki edit keeps the previous version, and anything can be restored.

## Where it is now

**Beta — free to use.**

Building worlds, co-writing, reading, canon voting, contribution rewards, and settlement records all work today.
Payment processing and real payouts aren't connected yet — for now, contribution points and settlement records accumulate.
